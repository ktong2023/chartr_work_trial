## 1. High: accidental accession collisions create unintended identity conflicts

`qa/build_task3.py:86` generates accession numbers from year/month and a hash reduced modulo 9,000. There is no uniqueness assertion for accession numbers. The current fixed fixture has four collisions between different patients:

| Accession | Patients | Dates |
|---|---|---|
| A2506-4839 | Tomas Villanueva / Abdul Lachance | June 22 / June 19, 2025 |
| A2506-9419 | Abdul Kowalski / Colin Kowalski | Both June 17, 2025 |
| A2603-6281 | Omar Wainwright / Kiara Gallagher | Both March 1, 2026 |
| A2508-9038 | Blake Caldwell / Alicia Ellison | Both August 4, 2025 |

Each has distinct specimens and accessioning records carrying different MRN/DOB identities. Three also share collection dates. For A2506-9419, both results are TP-PA, both accession entries have the same author and timestamp, and their patient identities differ. A model joining accession records by the documented accession value cannot establish a unique lab-side identity in that example.

These are not authored ambiguity cases: both Kowalski patients are expected to have `MISFILED_RESULT = not_an_issue`. The private rules engine reads generator ownership facts rather than reconstructing the rendered accession join, so its agreement with authored truth misses the defect. The collision also causes the evidence partner map to connect unrelated charts (for example, Tomas Villanueva and Abdul Lachance).

**Recommendation:** generate unique accession identifiers per intended specimen, assert that uniqueness during build, and check rendered Observation → Specimen → accession relationships independently. Preserve intentional multiple results from one specimen only through explicitly shared specimen identity. Regenerate the fixture and integrity baseline with a version increment after checking the intended clinical decisions remain unchanged.

## 3. Medium: the advertised stage-inference cases disclose their stage

All **13 F3 generated patients** have explicit staging in their clinical notes. `f3_stage_inference` calls `diagnose(..., recorded=False)`, but that switch only omits the Condition resource; it does not remove the stage from the note.

Examples include “Secondary syphilis,” “Latent syphilis, duration unknown,” and “Treating as late latent.” Patients g170–g174 directly name secondary syphilis; g175–g182 directly name late/unknown-duration latent disease.

These are genuine clinical statements, not private-answer leakage. However, the model can extract the stage instead of inferring it from examination/history. Downstream treatment and follow-up reasoning remains necessary.

**Recommendation:** if stage inference is the intended capability, author designated cases whose assessment genuinely leaves staging open while the examination and history support a unique stage. Retain explicitly diagnosed controls. Do not indiscriminately remove useful clinical assessments from every chart.

## 4. Medium: scale adds workload more clearly than demonstrated reasoning difficulty

The new families are a useful improvement: disputed identity can change another patient's follow-up; a pregnancy-test identity can affect treatment adequacy; date corrections alter interval calculations; outside records can change treatment anchors. Variants include both consequential and irrelevant uncertainties.

Nevertheless, most generated patients belong to a single family with at most one explicitly modeled unknown. Repeated short note templates remain common. After removing numbers and signatures, one late-latent intake template occurs 44 times, another 38 times, and two secondary-syphilis templates occur 27 and 24 times. Whole-chart uniqueness through dates, staff, names, and noise is not evidence of distinct reasoning structure.

This is not proof that a cheap heuristic solves the entire cohort, and scripting is explicitly allowed. It is a reason not to equate 300 patients with a tenfold increase in reasoning difficulty.

**Recommended direction after correctness fixes:** compose independent complications within selected histories, so resolving one fact changes the relevance of another; use matched counterfactuals in which changing one clinical fact changes the answer, while changing irrelevant wording or metadata does not. Test a public-record solver against held-out combinations or seeds. Retain realistic easy/control cases rather than making every unusual phrase signal an issue.
